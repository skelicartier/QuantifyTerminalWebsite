Best Bloomberg Terminal Alternatives in 2026

Bloomberg is still the benchmark, and at $31,980 a seat a year it is priced like one. These are the nine alternatives most teams shortlist, what each does best, what it costs and where it stops.

By Aaryan Saroha, Founder and CEO · Published 31 August 2026 · Updated 27 September 2026 · 6 min read

There is no single replacement for Bloomberg. LSEG Workspace and FactSet come closest across the whole terminal. Koyfin, TIKR and Morningstar cover company and fund research for far less. TradingView owns charting, OpenBB suits teams that build their own stack, S&P Capital IQ Pro leads on deals and private companies, and Quartr leads on earnings material.

What Bloomberg costs, and why teams look elsewhere

Bloomberg does not publish a rate card. Its last announced increase, 6.5% on two-year subscriptions renewing from 1 January 2025, put a single terminal at $31,980 a year and each terminal on a multi-terminal subscription at $28,320 [1]. Pricing trackers still showed $31,980 in August 2026 [2]. For a five-person desk that is about $141,600 a year before any other data. Our Bloomberg Terminal cost guide breaks the figures down.

Price is only part of it. Most users touch a fraction of the functions they pay for, years of keyboard shortcuts make switching slow, and much of the work still leaves the terminal for a spreadsheet model, a research note or a chat thread. Teams that look elsewhere usually want to pay for the part they use, and to stop re-keying the rest.

The nine alternatives

1. LSEG Workspace

The successor to Refinitiv Eikon and Bloomberg’s closest enterprise rival: cross-asset market data, Reuters news, analytics and Excel workflows, with the support a large organisation expects.

Where it stops: the same trade-offs as Bloomberg — contracts, entitlements, onboarding and a price set by quote. It makes sense when a team needs that breadth and can carry the overhead.

2. FactSet

A long-standing favourite of buy-side and sell-side research, known for clean fundamentals, estimates, ownership, portfolio analytics, attribution and reporting that feed straight into models.

Where it stops: it is sold to firms, not individuals, and priced for teams whose core work is deep fundamental research and portfolio reporting.

3. S&P Capital IQ Pro

Strongest for company analysis, comparable companies, transactions, private-company data, ownership and credit, and the Excel-heavy work of bankers and corporate development teams.

Where it stops: it is rarely the simplest or cheapest route for a small team that mainly needs market monitoring and portfolio work.

4. Koyfin

One of the strongest browser-based choices for fundamental investors: dashboards, financial statements, estimates, watchlists, macro views, charts and screens, with published tiers that make it easy to try.

Where it stops: it is a research and analytics product. Trading desks and quantitative teams usually need other tools around it.

5. TradingView

The default for chart-heavy work: charts, alerts, community indicators, screeners, broad market coverage and links to brokers. Many professionals use it even with a pricier terminal on the desk.

Where it stops: research depth. It is a charting layer, not a fundamental research or portfolio risk system.

6. TIKR

A strong low-cost option for company research across global equities: financial statements, estimates, ownership, screening and valuation.

Where it stops: it is focused on stocks, so it does not cover other asset classes, portfolio risk or trading.

7. Morningstar Investor

Useful for long-horizon investors who care about fund research, analyst ratings, valuation discipline and portfolio diagnostics, and far more approachable than an enterprise terminal.

Where it stops: it is a research and portfolio-quality tool, not a live market workstation.

8. OpenBB

Compelling when ownership and customisation matter: a research environment built around your own data sources, models and code, which suits engineering-led funds and data teams.

Where it stops: it rewards technical users. Teams that want a finished product may find it more setup than they expected.

9. Quartr

A specialist in earnings calls, transcripts, filings, slide decks and other investor-relations material, valuable when your edge comes from reading how management explains its numbers.

Where it stops: it complements a broader platform rather than replacing one.

Where Quantify Terminal fits

We build Quantify Terminal, so weigh this section accordingly. It is not a cheaper copy of Bloomberg’s data, and it does not replace Bloomberg’s messaging, fixed-income depth or execution. It does the part of the job most terminals leave to you: working out what new information means for the positions you hold.

Overnight, it reads the filings, prices and news that land against your book. By morning the brief says which documents touched which holdings, which assumption each one moved, and which held. When a filing changes a figure, the model change is proposed with the previous value beside the new one and the filing it came from, and nothing changes until you approve it. Your thesis is written as assumptions with conditions and re-checked when those figures move. What you decide is recorded with the evidence and model version on screen, so a year later you can show why you acted.

It runs on macOS, Windows and Linux, and is in early access for funds and investment teams, by application. See the product or request early access.

How to choose

  • The whole terminal, with budget no constraint: LSEG Workspace or FactSet.
  • Company research on a budget: Koyfin or TIKR.
  • Charts and alerts: TradingView.
  • Deals and private companies: S&P Capital IQ Pro.
  • Your own data and code: OpenBB.
  • Earnings material: Quartr, beside something broader.

Then test two or three against a real week: the morning scan, one company in depth, one portfolio review and one decision you have to write down. The tool that leaves the least re-keying is the cheaper one.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free Bloomberg Terminal alternative?

There are free tiers rather than free equivalents. Koyfin, TradingView and TIKR have free plans and OpenBB is open source; each covers one part of the work, and the limits show in data depth and delays.

How much does a Bloomberg Terminal cost in 2026?

About $31,980 a year for a single terminal, or $28,320 a terminal on a multi-terminal subscription, sold on two-year subscriptions. Bloomberg quotes each contract, so check yours.

Can an alternative fully replace Bloomberg?

For fixed income, Instant Bloomberg messaging and execution on its network, usually not. For public-equity research and portfolio monitoring, many teams cover most of their week with one or two of the tools above.

What suits a small fund?

One broad research tool, plus a specialist only where it clearly helps. Test each against a real week of work before signing a contract.
