How we handle data rights.

The institutional buyer's second question, answered in public: what you may see, what you may feed into a model, and how the system stops you from doing anything else.

Every source carries a licence profile

Every market observation, corporate fact, filing claim, event and private document in Quantify Terminal is stored with the source it came from and a machine-readable licence profile for that source. The profile records what is permitted: display, non-display, derived use, caching, redistribution and AI retrieval. Those permissions are enforced by the read layer itself, at retrieval time: the licence profile travels with the value, so every caller that reads a field — the panels, the research engine, the exporters, the MCP server an agent talks to — is answered through the same check. The desk never depends on which vendor supplied a field, and no caller can read past what the licence allows.

Display versus non-display

Display use is a person looking at data on a screen: a quote panel, a chart, a filing viewer. Non-display use is data consumed by a process rather than a person: fed into a model, a screen, a backtest, a risk calculation or an agent. Exchanges and data providers license the two differently, and many licence display use but restrict non-display use to separately entitled users. Quantify Terminal records which of the two each source permits and applies it. A field licensed for display only can be shown, but it will not be fed into a model, a screen or an agent.

What each licence tier permits

The free desktop terminal ships with delayed market data, core research, charting and portfolio tools. Delayed data is licensed for display and for the research and portfolio work that runs on the desk. Real-time and low-latency feeds carry per-user entitlement and reporting obligations, and they are part of the paid plans that open with the next release.

Why some data is delayed

Real-time redistribution of exchange data requires per-user entitlement, reporting back to the exchange, and in most cases a fee per user per month. Delayed data does not. Keeping an early-access licence on delayed data keeps it licence-clean for everyone who installs it, without a registration step that the data does not justify. When you move to a paid plan, the entitlement is recorded against your account and the real-time feed is switched on for you, not for the software in general.

How entitlements are enforced

Enforcement is not a setting. It sits in the layer every read goes through, so the desktop, a research run, an export and an agent calling the terminal as a tool are all answered by the same rules, and the rules are the source's own.

  • Each request is checked against the licence profile of every source it would touch.
  • A computed figure inherits the rights of what it was computed from. A ratio, a trailing-twelve-month line, a beta, a news tone or a last-reported date records the sources it was built on, and the answer for it is the strictest of theirs — so a multiple computed from a quote licensed for display only cannot be exported or placed in a model prompt, and the refusal names the source you would have to licence.
  • When a feed fails, Data Guardian fails over only to an alternate provider you are entitled to. It never borrows from one you are not.
  • For private documents, embedding access is document access. Vector indexes carry the same permissions as the documents they index, so nothing surfaces through an AI answer that a user could not open directly.
  • Every rights decision is recorded, allowed or refused, with the source, the use and the reason, in the terminal's own audit log.

What the system will not do

  • Show you data you are not licensed to see.
  • Feed display-only data into a model, screen, backtest or agent.
  • Retrieve a source for an AI answer where the licence forbids AI retrieval.
  • Cache or redistribute beyond what the licence permits.
